Ternary Training Map

Training a model whose weights are only {−1, 0, +1} with no full-precision copy of them (optimizer state at most sublinear in parameter count). What we tried, what it showed, what is running, what we have not tried and what is still open.

What TTF is: a short low-rank momentum (β 0.9) → Adam-normalised step → a long low-rank accumulator (leak ~1000 steps) → a ternary weight moves one level when its accumulated evidence crosses θ = 16, and the move is spent from the accumulator. Built from an ablation of master-weight training (2 Oct). Checked 3 Oct: rebuilt models reproduce their logged losses to <6e-5; no val article in train; 69 kernel tests pass; master's lr 1.5e-3 is the best of four; master is the BitNet b1.58 recipe (a two-stage schedule did not improve it); a ternary Bop baseline is 0.38 behind; storage formats: 8-bit direction + 16-bit accumulator free (both seeds); the lean recipe (int8 / fp16 state, bf16 / fp16 float tail, update inside the backward) 2.7020 vs 2.7067. Objections and evidence: docs/THREATS.md.

Memory, measured (micro-batch 1–2 × 2048, lean): 110M 0.38–0.63 GiB, 340M 0.68–1.35, 1.3B 1.61–2.97 (state on host / on GPU), 10B 12.3 GiB (16 GB card), 15B 17.0 GiB (24 GB card), the 26.5B preset 14.1 GiB at ranks 256 on a 16 GB card (24.6 s/step). Master + AdamW: 16 bytes per parameter before activations.

The story so far

1 · Momentum fixes

Momentum is noise-level (|M|/|g| ≈ 4.1); its flips are ~50% right
↓
20M screens: additive magnitude −0.052, sign gate −0.026
↓
vnorm, multiplicative, spend, refresh, stuck mask: no gain
↓
Additive r4 at 300M: 3.082; r16 + adapter weight decay + temperature: 2.9925

2 · Look-ahead and induction

No bend at 12M; no induction heads in any look-ahead run
↓
Without look-ahead: general induction from ~98M (3.05 at 295M; master 3.17)
↓
On natural text every run copies equally well; look-ahead runs only fail on random tokens
↓
Switching look-ahead on later = a constant loss gain, circuits kept; best schedule 3.111
↓
Look-ahead's per-step win was mostly 3× more data; per token processed, plain momentum is better

3 · Why momentum is not aligned

Weights frozen: momentum reaches 0.92 alignment in 33 batches (rank 256 loses nothing)
↓
With flips: +0.03–0.05 where flips are chosen
↓
One step of flips turns the true gradient to 0.45–0.82; the move overshoots (best at half of it)
↓
Mechanisms (V1, V2, your design) can't fix alignment at this step size; at ¼ rate V2 +0.17, yours +0.13, plain +0.10
↓
Valley test: no step-to-step zigzag (consecutive true gradients cos 0.51–0.85); momentum vs the 33-step mean starts at −0.23
↓
Oracle steps: flipping the largest entries of any direction raises loss, the true gradient most (+0.46 at ¼ step)
↓
Flip choice along the true gradient: random sign-aligned flips lower loss (−0.024 at 1 step); size +1.75, consistency / Adam +1.04, factored Adam +0.59
↓
Why: largest entries pile up (busiest 1% of rows get 36% of the flips); capping per row cuts the damage 8× (+0.21). Our rule (∝ |M|, capped) ≈ random
↓
Wave found: the true gradient swings with a ~6–8 step period (autocorrelation +0.69, +0.19, −0.20 at lags 1–3); the steady part is only 4% of its energy
↓
Momentum (memory ~33 steps) anti-predicts the next gradients: −0.17, −0.19, −0.14 at lags 1–3. It averages over 4–5 swings and lags half a swing behind
↓
Our flips make the swing: frozen weights, no swing (gradient cos 0.95 at every lag, momentum +0.81). At ¼ rate the swing is slower (period ~16–20) but as deep (−0.24)
↓
Period ∝ 1/√rate. Shorter memory (β 0.8) swings faster, not less; one speed divisor per layer changes nothing (flips 164k → 178k)
↓
Per weight: 45% of flips go uphill; momentum's sign is right on only 42% of weights; at a reversal it is 7× the gradient (~7 steps to turn); 22% never turn within 41 steps; 75% of the momentum's size is batch noise
↓
Sign gate (flip only where this batch agrees with M) removes the swing: −0.085 in 41 steps vs −0.011; Adam-style step −0.030; per-row speed −0.016
↓
The swing is also inside a from-scratch run (rc_s0 @5000: −0.27 at lag 5; momentum −0.15 to −0.20 vs the next gradients)
↓
Found: without look-ahead the gate and the Adam-style step were never applied (wired only into the look-ahead path)
↓
Gate in training: 3.117 (2 seeds), but plain at the same lower flip count gives 3.121: its gain is the rate
↓
Why they combine: the gate fixes the direction (52% → 39–46% uphill), the Adam step moves flips out of the steepest rows (27–33% → 19%)
↓
Adam-style step: 3.106 at the same flip count (real per-flip gain); with the gate 3.077 (0.64× flips; rate control running)

4 · Step size

¼ flip rate (branch): 0.16 better right away
↓
…then descends slower; the gap closes (0.161 → 0.125 over 58M), parallel to look-ahead
↓
Fewer flips drop bad and good flips alike; a selector keeps a 69%-downhill half (vs 54%)
↓
¼ rate ends at 3.155, accumulate-then-flip 3.2795: the schedule anneals the rate so the offset vanishes
↓
Adaptive flip rate (drives itself to ~×0.06): best mid-run (3.182 at 254M), ends 3.160 = plain

5 · Speed, gravity, your rule (29 Sep)

One speed reference per layer (speedref): 3.1315 = plain. It cannot see a swing that lives in a few directions
↓
Your rule (no friction, velocity cap on the whole vector, fixed-scale tanh flip chance): 2.5–4× the old rule per flip in 41 steps, but the swing stays unless the cap is ~1 gradient
↓
In training: cap 3 ahead of plain to ~150M, then starves of flips late: 3.217. Cap 1 loses the unigram/bigram statistics (no-context loss rises 6.51 → 6.81): no dive
↓
Asymmetric gravity (momentum decays 0.5 when climbing): best per-weight turning in tests, 3.50 in training (both alone and with gate + Adam)
↓
Dry friction (fixed amount off per step), per vector or per weight: same as the tanh flip chance alone; swing unchanged
↓
Undo (flip back last step's flips the fresh batch and the momentum call uphill): with the gate it reverses 2% of flips and changes nothing; training run stopped
↓
Master has the same per-weight picture: 46.5% of trit changes uphill, every step overshoots its own direction. It differs by no lag-3 reversal and 12% never-turning weights (ours 22%)
↓
Where the gap to master is: local context (positions 2–15: +0.5), not induction. Gate + Adam gets induction late (~180M) and it flickers on and off in every flip run

6 · Memory length (29–30 Sep)

Sharp without look-ahead (gate + Adam step + additive r16 + weight decay + head temperature): 2.9875, beats look-ahead + sharp
↓
Where the gap is: split by how often the (previous, target) pair occurs in training, the gap sits in pairs seen < 10k times (+0.27 to +0.42); frequent pairs +0.07
↓
Why: a pair seen 100 times turns up once every ~120 steps; the momentum forgets in ~33. Not rank: 78–89% of those gradients lie in the rank-256 subspace
↓
Longer memory: decay 0.995 2.878, dry friction (β 1, ‖M‖ −= ‖g‖/33 per step) 2.881. Both have an optimum; β 1 alone 3.047 (never forgets), dry 0.1 3.003
↓
Rare-pair gap halved; long-memory runs form induction heads (copy gain +1.6 to +5.9; master +3.15)
↓
Dry friction = a decay that sets its own memory: 220 → 390 steps over the run; ties the best fixed decay
↓
With a long memory rank matters: 256 → 512 −0.046, → 1024 −0.016. Spend adds −0.01
↓
2.8198 (dry + rank 1024), 2.8215 (dry + spend + rank 512): ppl 16.8 vs master 15.7
↓
No gain: dither, slow gate, dry friction per weight, undo
